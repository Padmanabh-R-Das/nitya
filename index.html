Nitya
The daily duty that gets done

Planning is free. Execution is the scarce thing.

Nitya is an execution layer for people carrying several tracks at once. It owns the schedule, decides each day against what actually happened yesterday, moves missed work forward instead of piling it, and reports completion honestly.

Running daily since March 2026 · solo founder · second instance now in daily use

The loop, every day

The user makes no planning decisions and maintains no calendar. These five steps run whether or not anyone opens the page.

06:11

Agent wakes

Reads yesterday's marks, the calendar, deadlines and anything that slipped.

06:11

Re-plans

Rebuilds the day. Missed work moves forward; it is never doubled or stacked.

06:11

Asks, narrowly

At most two closed questions a day, with buttons. Never an open prompt.

All day

User executes

Blocks and problems are marked done, done-with-help, or missed.

Weekly

Verdict

Completion stated as a number, unsoftened, and next week is cut to fit.

It researches, then changes its own plan

Nitya does not only schedule what it was told. A weekly research pass reads across the user's stated goals and interest domains — opportunities, deadlines, contests, programmes, market and hiring signals — vets what it finds against several sources, and recalibrates the plan and the targets when the evidence warrants it. Every such change is surfaced on the interface with its reason, so the user can see what moved and why.

Intake

Sources, vetted

Subscribed channels, official pages, programme and contest calendars, mail. Several sources are cross-read to separate substance from marketing.

Recalibration

Goals move, not just tasks

A newly found deadline, a changed requirement or a dead end rewrites the targets themselves, not merely the week's blocks.

Transparency

Every change is shown

Plan changes appear on the dashboard with their reason and status, and anything needing a human decision is escalated rather than assumed.

What it plans across

One engine, several life tracks, each with its own ladder, targets and review cadence. The engine is domain-agnostic; the opinions are per track.

Under the surface

Scheduled agents

Nine recurring runs

A daily planning agent, a weekly review, several domain research desks, a mail triage, a usage steward. Model tiering keeps cost per user low: a small model daily, a larger one weekly.

State

Versioned document store

Plan, decisions, directives, assumptions, risks and logs. Every write is version-pinned, so a background agent and a human edit cannot overwrite each other.

Interface

Dashboard, not an inbox

Today's plan, the practice ladder, the long-range roadmap, the record. Closed-question inputs with buttons; a decision queue for anything needing a human.

Guardrails

Built against dependence

A weekly timed session with no AI assistance and a monthly defend-your-own-work drill, both scored, so capability is measured rather than assumed.

Reporting

Three channels

A daily one-line note, a weekly summary with the plain numbers, and a message whenever a decision is genuinely required.

Honesty

No congratulation

The weekly number is reported as measured. A failed week shrinks the next plan instead of repeating it.

Why this should work

The two mechanisms are not invented. Both have been measured across large bodies of published research.

d = 0.65effect of if-then implementation plans over goal intentions alone, across 94 studies (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006) — Nitya makes every plan concrete and timed
d = 0.40behaviour change from increased progress monitoring, across 138 interventions (Harkin et al., 2016) — Nitya monitors and reports by default
~50%of intentions translate into action (Sheeran & Webb, 2016) — the gap the product exists to close
